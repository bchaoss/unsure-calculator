A calculator for numbers with uncertainty. Combine uncertain ranges with standard numbers in your everyday calculations.

How to use

Use ~ to include uncertain ranges in your expressions, like 10~20 * 4. Here, 10~20 represents a 95% interval range under a normal distribution. The ~ symbol is available on the keypad.

You can also use +- to specify a center value and its 95% uncertainty interval, such as 100 (+-10) or 100 (+-10%).

The calculator returns a range of possible results, also expressed as a 95% interval. Results also display a probability distribution chart and a percentiles table.

Examples

Here are some ideas on how to use this calculator (adapted from the original project):

  • Estimate viability of a business idea, with uncertain size of the market, market share, monthly sales per person, and operational costs: 50000~80000 × 0.10~0.20 × 5~10 - 20000~50000
  • Estimate future income with uncertain money per month, length of a gig, and tax rate: 1000~1500 × 10~12 × (30~50 / 100)
  • Estimate yearly time saved by a dishwasher (or any other piece of technology) given uncertain number of times used per week, time saving per use, and installation costs: (4~7 × 10~20 × 51) / 60 - 2~4
  • Estimate total return of an investment account. Both the interest rate and length of investing is unsure: 5000 × -2~5% × 5~10
  • Estimate the probability of dying in a pandemic, given an uncertain morbidity rate (how many people get sick) and mortality rate (how many infected people die): (10~30 / 100) × (0.1~1.0 / 100)
  • Estimate the height of a skyscraper, given an uncertain distance from its base, and an uncertain angle in which we see the top of it: 90~100 × tan(70~80)
  • Estimate return on investment of a marketing campaign, given uncertain views, click through rate, conversion rate, revenue per order, and ad spend: (1M × 2~3% × 3~5% × 10~15 - 2~3K) / 2~3K

Functions and limitations

Standard operations (+, -, ×, /) and ~ are built into the keypad. Other functions can also be input with uncertainty, such as sqrt(10~20), (10~20) ^ 2 (or ** for exponents), and sin(0~1) (cos, tan).

Units of a range are supported: K, M, B, T, and %. For example, 4~6K applies thousand to both numbers, meaning 4000~6000.

Precedence: The ~ operator has higher precedence than other operations, if parentheses are omitted.

Limitations:

  • The default range represents a 95% interval under a normal distribution. (Support for other uncertainty levels & distributions is planned for the future.)
  • Calculations are using the Monte Carlo simulation, which may take a few seconds to compute.
